Wingstop Nutrition and Calories

Updated 25 November 2026 · By WingstopMenus.us · Contact: contact@wingstopmenus.us

Wingstop food is fried, sauced, and filling, so it is worth knowing what you are eating before you order. The good news is that the numbers are simple: a plain wing is about 90 calories, the flavor adds a little, and the sides and drinks add the most. This page explains the numbers from the official Wingstop Nutritional Guide of April 2025, and shows how to build a lighter order. For the full tables, see the Latest Wingstop menu information on nutrition.

Calories in a wing

A plain classic wing has about 90 calories and 10 grams of protein. That is the baseline. Sauce or dry rub adds a little more, so a flavored classic wing is about 90 to 120 calories depending on the flavor. A boneless wing is about 80 to 110 calories.

Those numbers are per wing, so the total depends on how many you eat. Six classic wings are roughly 540 to 720 calories before sides. Ten classic wings are roughly 900 to 1,200 calories. That is a full meal on its own, before fries or a drink.

Tenders and the chicken sandwich sit in the same range, because they use the same chicken and the same flavors. A tender is roughly the size of two wings, so count each tender as about two wings when you add up the meal. The sandwich is one fillet on a bun, so its total depends more on the bun and the sauce than on the chicken itself.

What adds the most calories

The wings are not the biggest calorie source in most orders. The sides and extras are. A large order of seasoned fries adds several hundred calories, cheese sauce and ranch add more, and a large sugary drink can add as much as a few wings. If you want a lighter meal, the sides are the first place to look.

Veggie sticks are the lightest side on the menu. Swapping fries for veggie sticks, or ordering a small fries instead of a large, cuts more calories than switching wing flavors. Ordering water instead of a sugary drink helps too, and it costs nothing.

Protein, fat, and sodium

Wings are a high-protein food. A plain classic wing carries about 10 grams of protein, so a 10-piece order gives you about 100 grams of protein. That is more than most people need in one meal, which is why wings work well for people who track protein.

Fat and sodium are the other side of the picture. Fried wings carry a fair amount of fat, and the sauces and rubs add sodium. The exact numbers vary by flavor, and the nutrition page lists them item by item. If sodium matters to you, plain wings with a light dip are the safest pick.

Allergens and gluten

Two flavors contain gluten as an ingredient: Hawaiian and Spicy Korean Q. Wingstop also says it does not claim any product is gluten-free, because all fried foods are cooked in the same oil. If you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy, that matters more than the ingredient list of one flavor.

Shared fryers are the main cross-contact risk. Fries, wings, and tenders all go through the same oil, so a fried item is never safe to call gluten-free, even when its own ingredients have no gluten. Check the allergen page before you order if this applies to you.

How to build a lighter order

Start with plain or mildly flavored wings, keep the piece count at six or eight, swap fries for veggie sticks, pick water instead of a sugary drink, and go easy on the cheese sauce. That order still tastes like Wingstop, and it cuts several hundred calories from a standard combo.

Questions people ask

How many calories are in a Wingstop wing?

A plain classic wing has about 90 calories and 10 grams of protein. A flavored classic wing is about 90 to 120 calories, and a boneless wing is about 80 to 110 calories.

What is the lowest-calorie Wingstop order?

Six plain classic wings with veggie sticks and water is one of the lightest orders on the menu. Avoid large fries, cheese sauce, and sugary drinks if you want to keep calories down.

Is Wingstop gluten-free?

No. Wingstop does not claim any product is gluten-free, and all fried foods are cooked in the same oil. The Hawaiian and Spicy Korean Q flavors contain gluten as an ingredient.
